# 01 — Business discovery and feasibility

**Status:** Preliminary; site validation pending  
**Project:** Chirackal Ayurveda Hospital & Pharmacy, Muttom, Thodupuzha, Idukki, Kerala

## 1. Engagement brief

The client operates an Ayurveda healthcare establishment with a pharmacy. The requested outcome is a complete digital transformation covering the public website, internal hospital operations, pharmacy operations and patient-facing digital services.

## 2. Confirmed discovery facts

| Topic | Confirmed |
|---|---|
| Engagement | Client project |
| Business scope | Hospital and pharmacy |
| Current recordkeeping | Paper-based |
| Transformation goal | Complete digital transformation |
| Inpatient admission | No inpatient facility |
| Clinical service model | Consultations and Ayurveda treatments/therapies |
| Pharmacy model | Both hospital-linked dispensing and retail pharmacy activity |

The phrase “no inpatient” means the initial scope should not assume bed allocation, admission, ward rounds, inpatient medication charts or discharge workflows. Whether day-care treatment sessions are offered, and their operational details, still needs confirmation.

The pharmacy clarification means the system must support both medicine dispensing connected to a consultation/prescription and independent retail transactions. The client must confirm whether these are one legal business and stock location or separate counters, licences, books or premises.

## 3. Business model to validate

### Hospital
- Outpatient registration and patient identification.
- Appointment and walk-in handling.
- Practitioner consultation and clinical documentation.
- Prescription creation and follow-up.
- Practitioner-prescribed treatment plans and therapy sessions.
- Billing and collection.

### Pharmacy
- Prescription-linked dispensing.
- Independent retail sale.
- Medicine catalogue and pricing.
- Supplier purchases and receiving.
- Batch, expiry and stock movement control.
- Returns, adjustments and daily reconciliation.

### Shared operations
- Patient identity and contact details.
- Staff identity and access.
- Cash/card/UPI payment recording.
- Receipts and financial summaries.
- Notifications and patient communication.
- Management reporting and audit history.

## 4. Feasibility questions

Before estimating cost or committing to implementation, collect:
- Staff count and job responsibilities.
- Daily/weekly patient and therapy volumes.
- Number of practitioners and their schedules.
- Number of therapy rooms and treatment resources.
- Pharmacy product count, batch tracking practice and average daily bills.
- Current registers, forms, invoice formats and accounting process.
- Hardware, internet reliability, power backup and printer availability.
- Applicable registrations, licences and accounting/GST requirements.
- Budget, delivery expectations, support ownership and data migration needs.

## 5. Value hypotheses (not yet measured)

1. Faster patient lookup and less repeated registration.
2. More consistent consultation and prescription records.
3. Better coordination between treatment scheduling and practitioner instructions.
4. More reliable pharmacy stock, batch and expiry visibility.
5. Fewer manual billing calculations and improved daily reconciliation.
6. Better follow-up communication and local online discoverability.

Baseline metrics must be measured before claiming improvements.

## 6. Feasibility conclusion

A phased, shared-data platform is a reasonable direction. Start with a responsive staff web application and public website. Add native patient/staff apps only after validating use cases, adoption and maintenance costs. No final technology, price, timeline or feature commitment should be treated as approved until the site discovery and client sign-off are complete.
